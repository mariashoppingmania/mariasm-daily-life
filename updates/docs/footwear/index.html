01 / THE WORLDWIDE STORY

A history written in footsteps.

Footwear history has many overlapping timelines. This selection is international and avoids treating any culture, century or fashion movement as a single uniform style.

CHAPTER 01 · Ancient worlds · prehistory to 500 BCE

Protection, climate, identity

From plant fibre and hide coverings to sandals and sewn shoes, early footwear responded to terrain, weather and local resources. Survival of archaeological examples is uneven: the history we can see is shaped by what endured.

Study: V&A footwear timeline

CHAPTER 02 · Egypt, Greece and Rome

Sandals and social meaning

Ancient Mediterranean societies used sandals made from leather and plant fibres. Elite status, labour, military requirements and local climate influenced construction. There was no single ancient shoe worn by everybody.

Study: The Met footwear collection

CHAPTER 03 · South, Central and East Asia

Local ingenuity, varied traditions

Wooden geta and zōri in Japan, embroidered slippers in China, leather jutti and mojari in South Asia and other regional footwear reveal different histories. Styles changed across generations and communities; avoid reducing each region to one stereotype.

Study: V&A global collections

CHAPTER 04 · Northern Eurasia and Indigenous traditions

Insulation and mobility

Moccasins, fur boots, felt and layered footwear reflect diverse local techniques. Communities adapted stitching, materials and fit to seasonal conditions. Cultural designs have specific histories rather than being generic fashion motifs.

Study: museum collections

CHAPTER 05 · 1400–1700 · Europe

Chopines, pattens and raised soles

Raised platforms, including Venetian chopines, reflected fashion, status and the practical need to protect footwear from dirty streets. Extreme silhouettes were culturally situated and not everyday dress for everyone.

V&A historical collections

CHAPTER 06 · 1700s

Buckles, embroidery and court display

Fine textiles, leather, embroidery and ornate buckles appeared in elite fashion. Shoemakers built distinctive lasts and heels, while everyday footwear followed very different economic and practical priorities.

The Met Costume Institute

CHAPTER 07 · 1800–1890

Factories change the shoe

Industrial manufacturing, standardised sizing and specialist shops expanded footwear access. Leather boots and buttoned or laced shoes coexisted with formal and functional types.

Shoemakers Museum

CHAPTER 08 · 1900–1920s

Lace-up boots give way to dance shoes

Early twentieth-century fashion moved between practical boots, low heels and increasingly visible straps. In the 1920s, T-bar and Mary Jane-inspired shoes suited changing hemlines and dance culture.

Fashion museum references

CHAPTER 09 · 1930–1950s

Hollywood glamour and ingenious soles

Elegant pumps, wedges and platform experiments reflected design innovation and wartime material constraints. Salvatore Ferragamo's work is an important chapter, but not the whole history of the wedge.

The Met Costume Institute

CHAPTER 10 · 1950–1960s

Sneakers, stilettos and youth style

Sports footwear entered casual wardrobes, while sharply sculpted heels became a fashion focus. Teenage culture and new media helped popularise new silhouettes.

The Met Costume Institute

CHAPTER 11 · 1970s

Platforms, subcultures and comfort

High soles, clogs, athletic shapes and boots expressed disco, rock, punk and everyday comfort. Subcultures used footwear as a statement of belonging.

V&A history timeline

CHAPTER 12 · 1980–1990s

The sneaker becomes an icon

Basketball shoes, running technology, streetwear and global brands turned sneakers into cultural objects. Chunky trainers, combat boots and minimal pumps all flourished.

V&A + brand histories

CHAPTER 13 · 2000–2010s

From ballet flats to the sneaker boom

The 2000s mixed pointy-toe shoes, platforms and ballet flats. The 2010s brought renewed interest in heritage sneakers, slides, minimalist trainers and experimental heels.

Museum and brand archives

CHAPTER 14 · 2020–2026

Personal style over one uniform trend

Retro football shoes, chunky and slim sneakers, loafers, Mary Janes, outdoor-inspired boots, ballet flats and comfortable sandals coexist. Materials, provenance and repairability are increasingly part of shopping decisions.

Contemporary product catalogues

Historical sources: the V&A holds around 2,000 pairs covering more than three millennia, while The Metropolitan Museum of Art documents innovations in twentieth-century soles, materials and heel engineering. Sources are linked at the end of this guide.

02 / THE STYLE ENCYCLOPAEDIA

Sixteen silhouettes, countless personalities.

From ballet flats and Mary Janes to rugged boots and sleek sneakers. Learn what each type is, when it works and which design details matter for fit.

Everyday / streetwear

Sneakers

Low- and high-top trainers in leather, suede, textile, mesh or mixed materials. Compare outsole grip, toe room and cushioning to your intended use.

Smart casual / tailoring

Loafers

Slip-on shoes with apron seams, saddle or hardware details. Penny, horsebit and chunky interpretations differ in structure.

Minimal / romantic

Ballet flats

Light slip-ons with rounded, almond or pointed toes. Very thin soles are a style choice, not ideal for every foot or all-day activity.

Classic / vintage

Mary Janes

Shoes with instep straps, from flat school-inspired versions to stacked-heel and platform designs.

Formal / heritage

Oxfords & derbies

Closed lacing in Oxfords, open lacing in derbies. Brogue perforations can decorate either construction.

Dress / occasion

Pumps & stilettos

Closed-front heels in kitten, block, cone or stiletto formats. Heel height and toe box shape influence comfort.

Height / statement

Wedges & platforms

Wedges support a large part of the foot; platforms lift the forefoot as well. Stable designs still require careful walking.

Autumn / versatile

Ankle & Chelsea boots

Mid-height boots with zips, laces or elastic gussets. Look for shaft fit, tread and waterproof construction if relevant.

Winter / fashion

Tall boots

Knee-high and over-the-knee silhouettes in smooth leather, stretch fabrics and suede. Shaft width matters.

Summer / vacation

Sandals

Slides, straps, fisherman and ankle-wrap shapes. Secure straps and a grippy sole can matter on longer walks.

Easy slip-on

Mules & clogs

Open-back or partially enclosed shoes, often in leather, suede, felt, cork or moulded synthetics.

Summer / Mediterranean

Espadrilles

Textile uppers with traditionally rope-like soles, sometimes reinforced with rubber. Avoid assuming all styles are rainproof.

Activity-specific

Running / trail shoes

Engineered mesh, protective overlays and specialised soles. Choose shoes for the activity, not just appearance.

Protection / outdoor

Hiking & work boots

Sturdy uppers, deeper tread, sometimes protective toes or membranes. Safety certification matters for real work environments.

Culture / craftsmanship

Traditional footwear

Geta, zōri, jutti, huaraches, moccasins and others represent distinct contexts. Research origin and artisans rather than treating them as costumes.

At home

Slippers & house shoes

Soft textiles, felt and lined designs; choose non-slip bottoms for smooth indoor floors.

LEARN TO READ A SILHOUETTE

Shape changes everything.

A rounded toe, low shaft and flat sole creates a different feeling from a pointed pump or tall boot. Watch the balance between toe width, heel shape, upper flexibility and the occasion.

Try matching one shoe to two contrasting outfits: a tailored trouser and a relaxed denim look, for example. The effect depends as much on proportion and texture as on colour.

03 / SHOES AND CULTURE

Footwear across the world.

Eight starting points for appreciating craftsmanship and context. Designs frequently move between cultures, but their historical origins should still be acknowledged.

REGION 01

Europe

Italian leather craft, Spanish espadrille making, British shoemaking traditions, Venetian platforms and modern luxury design all represent different regional histories.

REGION 02

Japan

Geta and zōri are tied to specific clothing and events; modern Japanese sneaker, street-fashion and designer culture is equally diverse.

REGION 03

South Asia

Jutti and mojari traditions include leather, embroidery and regional variations; their craftsmanship deserves specific attribution.

REGION 04

East and Southeast Asia

Footwear spans historic wooden, textile and leather forms as well as contemporary urban shoe industries. Never assume a uniform 'Asian' style.

REGION 05

Africa

Local sandals, leatherwork, beadwork and contemporary designers vary profoundly by country and community; historical and modern forms coexist.

REGION 06

The Americas

Indigenous moccasin and sandal traditions, cowboy boots, athletic innovations and global streetwear illustrate multiple distinct histories.

REGION 07

Middle East

Leather sandals, embroidered slippers and changing urban fashion reveal diversity in climate, trade, craft and social setting.

REGION 08

Oceania

Footwear shaped by island climates and local traditions exists alongside contemporary fashion and athletic culture.

04 / THE MATERIALS LIBRARY

What our shoes are made of.

Every material has trade-offs. Marketing words do not tell the whole story: construction, wear, repair, comfort and care are just as important.

MATERIAL 01 · Natural hide

Full-grain & smooth leather

Can mould with wear; finish and tanning influence durability and breathability. Condition as appropriate, and check sourcing claims.

MATERIAL 02 · Napped leather

Suede

Soft and elegant; typically vulnerable to moisture and stains. Use a dedicated suede brush and suitable protector.

MATERIAL 03 · Fine sanded leather

Nubuck

Velvety texture with a shorter nap than many suedes; requires gentle cleaning and regular attention.

MATERIAL 04 · Coated glossy finish

Patent leather

Shiny and often easy to wipe; coating may crack with age, heat or poor storage.

MATERIAL 05 · Woven textile

Canvas & cotton

Light and versatile; absorbency, airflow and washing suitability vary by construction.

MATERIAL 06 · Technical textile

Mesh & engineered knit

Ventilation and flexible fit; abrasion resistance, lining and structure vary considerably.

MATERIAL 07 · PU/PVC-based

Synthetic leather

Often easier to wipe and sometimes lower-cost, but lifespan and breathability depend on quality and coating.

MATERIAL 08 · Cushioning polymers

EVA & foam

Lightweight cushioning used in midsoles and moulded shoes. Compression and wear change performance over time.

MATERIAL 09 · Outsoles

Natural & synthetic rubber

Durability and traction depend on compound and tread, not simply the word rubber.

MATERIAL 10 · Structural / natural fibres

Cork, jute & wood

Popular in footbeds, wedges, rope soles and clogs; construction and sealing affect maintenance.

MATERIAL 11 · Reprocessed inputs

Recycled materials

Recycled content can be valuable but does not automatically guarantee lower total impact or recyclability of the finished shoe.

MATERIAL 12 · Fasteners and bonds

Hardware & adhesives

Zips, buckles, eyelets, welts and glue influence repairability, serviceability and overall lifespan.

07 / THE PRACTICAL SHOE GUIDE

Fit, care and longevity.

Beautiful shoes should still suit their purpose. Use these checks to avoid mistakes when buying online.

Measure your feet

Stand on paper near the end of the day. Trace both feet, measure heel to longest toe and compare with the maker's latest chart. Check width and socks; EU sizes are not identical in fit across brands.

Walk before deciding

Allow toe space, examine pressure around the little toe and check heel movement. A shoe should not require painful breaking-in or cause tingling.

Care by material

Dust leather and condition according to its finish; brush suede when dry; wash textile only when manufacturer guidance permits. Let soaked shoes dry away from direct heat.

Rotate and maintain

Let damp shoes air, remove stones from tread and check soles before they become unsafe. A cobbler may repair heels, stitching or certain soles.

Buy with transparency

Check seller identity, authentic photos, return conditions, genuine sizing charts, duties and shipping. A promotional banner is not proof of stock.

Match shoes to activity

Running, wet pavements and formal occasions have different requirements. A fashion platform is not protective work footwear, and a fashion sneaker is not necessarily a running shoe.

Comfort and health: this is shopping guidance, not a medical or podiatry assessment. Persistent foot pain, wounds or numbness warrant professional advice.

10 / YOUR QUESTIONS

Helpful answers before buying.

Quick decisions on size, materials, construction, comfort and trustworthy e-commerce.

How do I choose my correct EU size?

Measure both feet while standing near the end of the day. Compare the longer foot with each brand's size chart, allowing toe room and checking width, heel movement and socks.

Are sneakers always comfortable?

No. Comfort depends on fit, construction, the activity and the person. Popularity and cushioning claims cannot replace trying the shoe.

Can I clean suede with water?

Avoid soaking. Let dirt dry, use a clean suede brush and follow the maker's material-specific instructions. Patch-test suitable protectors.

Are vegan leather shoes more sustainable?

Not automatically. Synthetic coatings, durability, manufacturing and end-of-life all influence impacts. Read the actual composition.

What is the difference between a welted and glued sole?

A welt is a stitched connecting strip used in some constructions; cemented shoes rely primarily on adhesive. Repairability depends on the exact build.

Should I break in tight shoes?

Some uppers soften, but persistent pressure, numbness or painful rubbing signals a poor fit. Avoid counting on excessive stretching.

Are stilettos bad for everyone?

Higher or narrower heels can increase forefoot pressure and affect balance. Risk depends on design, duration, mobility and personal health; comfort should guide use.

Do I need special shoes for running?

For repeated running, choose purpose-built models suited to fit, terrain and training rather than a fashion-only sneaker.

How can I check a retailer?

Review the legal business identity, delivery terms, return policy, secure checkout, genuine brand pages and suspiciously steep discounts.

Will prices on this page stay accurate?

No. Verified price snapshots are dated 31 October 2026 and indicative ranges are not quotes. Confirm exact colour, size, stock, shipping and promotions at checkout.
