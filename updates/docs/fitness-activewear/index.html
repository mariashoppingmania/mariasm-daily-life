01 · CULTURE & EVOLUTION

How fashion changed over time.

Exploring social context, technology and personal freedom across different decades. Not everyone in a period or culture dressed alike.

ERA 01 · Late 1800s–early 1900s

Late 1800s–early 1900s

Women's participation in sport helped challenge restrictive dress codes; bloomers and specialised garments appeared in particular settings.

ERA 02 · 1920–1940s

1920–1940s

Tennis, swimming and gymnastics clothing became more adaptable, though sport and social expectations differed across societies.

ERA 03 · 1950–1960s

1950–1960s

Knitted tops, leisure separates and increasingly specialised athletics clothing paved the way for a wider active wardrobe.

ERA 04 · 1970s

1970s

Aerobics, dance and recreational fitness popularised unitards, leotards and stretch fabrics in consumer culture.

ERA 05 · 1980s

1980s

Bright leotards, leg warmers and coordinated fitness sets shaped the decade's highly visible gym aesthetic.

ERA 06 · 1990s

1990s

Running gear, athletic tracksuits and technical sportswear broadened casual everyday wardrobes.

ERA 07 · 2000s

2000s

Low-rise yoga pants, velour tracksuits and new performance fibres crossed between studio and street.

ERA 08 · 2010s

2010s

Athleisure became a major fashion direction; leggings and sports bras gained broader everyday styling roles.

ERA 09 · 2020–2026

2020–2026

Seamless knits, sculpted sets, unitards, flared leggings, relaxed layers and soft earth tones coexist with bold colour.

03 · TEXTILES AND CONSTRUCTION

Know what you're wearing.

How fibre blends, fit and fabric finishes change comfort, longevity and care requirements.

Polyester

Common in moisture-management fabrics; treatments and knit structure determine performance.

Polyamide

Often soft and resilient in stretch garments; may feel different from polyester.

Elastane

Adds stretch and recovery; avoid excessive tumble-dryer heat.

Cotton

Comfortable for low-intensity use, but absorbs moisture and may feel damp during vigorous activity.

Merino blends

Sometimes used for temperature comfort and odour management; care and wool sensitivity vary.

Seamless knit

Continuous-knit engineering can reduce some seam irritation but is not inherently better for every wearer.

Compression panels

Supportive-feeling construction; fashion compression should not be confused with prescribed medical compression.

Mesh and perforation

Ventilating zones; verify opacity, abrasion resistance and comfort.

Recycled fibres

Read exact percentages and independent certifications; no automatic guarantee of low impact.

Material transparency: care, safety and environmental impact depend on the specific fabric and manufacture. Broad marketing claims such as “sustainable”, “sculpting” or “supportive” should always be checked against actual product details.

04 · THE PRACTICAL WARDROBE GUIDE

Fit, confidence and care.

Practical advice for choosing, styling and caring for your garments while respecting personal comfort.

Match support to movement

Running and jumping often need more bra support than gentle yoga. Try a brief movement test and avoid painful restriction.

Check squat opacity

Look at the fabric under daylight and while bending. A well-fitting garment should provide the coverage you prefer.

Avoid painful compression

Compression should not cause numbness, breathlessness or painful tightness. Medical compression is different.

Select for weather

Mesh and lightweight layers may suit heat; use wind or rain-resistant outerwear when conditions require it.

Keep waistbands stable

Bend, squat and walk to check rolling, sliding, bunching and the cut of shorts.

Make a capsule outfit

Start with one neutral leggings, one appropriate bra, two tops and one outer layer; add colours and silhouettes as desired.

Wash technical fabric correctly

Follow labels, wash promptly after workouts, and avoid high heat or products that impair fabric performance.

Respect personal style

Tight jumpsuits, crop tops or short shorts are clothing choices—not prerequisites for fitness or signs of ability.

LOOKBOOK NOTES

Feel confident through movement.

Match fitted leggings with a breathable crop tee or coordinated bra, then add a lightweight zip-up for changing weather. Prioritise appropriate support, movement and breathable fabrics over any particular aesthetic.

07 · HELPFUL QUESTIONS

Answers without fashion myths.

Common fit, materials and shopping questions answered clearly.

How do I choose a sports bra?

Try the bra for your movement and fit: check band, straps, cup coverage and any painful pressure. Higher impact may need more support.

What does squat-proof mean?

A marketing term for opaque coverage during bending. Test the actual garment and lighting; it is not a universal certification.

Are compressive leggings medical compression?

No. Fashion and sports compression does not substitute for prescribed compression garments.

Can a tight jumpsuit be used for sport?

Only if it allows safe range of motion, comfortable breathing and appropriate support. Test squats, stretching and mobility.

Should I wear cotton for high-sweat workouts?

Cotton can absorb sweat and remain damp. Technical fabrics may dry faster, though individual comfort varies.

What is the difference between shorts and biker shorts?

Biker shorts are generally close-fit and stretchy; running or training shorts can be woven and looser.

Do colours affect fitness performance?

Colours can influence personal motivation or preference, but no particular outfit colour is a substitute for training, rest or hydration.

How should I wash technical leggings?

Check the label; cool washing, air drying and avoiding excessive heat generally help preserve elastane.
