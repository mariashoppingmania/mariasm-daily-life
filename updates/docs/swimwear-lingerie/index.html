01 · CULTURE & EVOLUTION

How fashion changed over time.

Exploring social context, technology and personal freedom across different decades. Not everyone in a period or culture dressed alike.

ERA 01 · Ancient cultures

Ancient cultures

Swimwear as a modern category did not exist in one universal form. People swam, bathed and dressed for water according to culture, context and local custom.

ERA 02 · 1800s bathing costumes

1800s bathing costumes

In parts of Europe and North America, bathing dresses and layered garments reflected social expectations and beach conventions; practices varied greatly.

ERA 03 · 1900–1920

1900–1920

Swimwear began to streamline as swimming gained popularity and textile technology changed. Annette Kellerman became a symbol of more functional one-piece suits.

ERA 04 · 1930–1940

1930–1940

Knitted and woven suits became more fitted. The modern bikini was introduced in 1946, but adoption differed across countries and beaches.

ERA 05 · 1950–1960

1950–1960

Structured cups, prints and fashion magazines influenced resort wear. Two-piece sets and glamorous one-pieces coexisted.

ERA 06 · 1970s

1970s

Stretch textiles and high-cut silhouettes helped broaden options for movement, tanning and fashion.

ERA 07 · 1980–1990s

1980–1990s

High legs, bright colours, logo styling and the Brazilian-cut bottom appeared widely in popular culture and swim fashion.

ERA 08 · 2000–2010s

2000–2010s

Mix-and-match separates, bandeau tops, resort cover-ups and body-positive sizing conversations grew in visibility.

ERA 09 · 2020–2026

2020–2026

Ribbed fabrics, minimal silhouettes, high-waisted and thong bottoms, active surf suits and retro prints share the spotlight; there is no single compulsory style.

From bikinis to beautiful lingerie.

03 · TEXTILES AND CONSTRUCTION

Know what you're wearing.

How fibre blends, fit and fabric finishes change comfort, longevity and care requirements.

Polyamide / nylon

Stretchy swim fabrics commonly use nylon blends; check chlorine tolerance and drying instructions.

Polyester

Used in swimwear and lingerie; tends to retain colour differently depending on construction.

Elastane

Adds stretch and recovery; avoid excess heat and aggressive chlorine exposure.

Textured rib knit

Decorative rib structure; check opacity when wet and lining quality.

Cotton and modal

Common for breathable everyday lingerie and gussets; typically not ideal for swimming.

Lace and tulle

Decorative, often delicate; avoid snagging and follow label care.

Silk and satin

Luxury drape and sheen; satin is a weave, not automatically silk.

Recycled fibres

Check exact composition and credible claims; recycled content alone does not guarantee low environmental impact.

UPF fabrics

Some fabrics are tested to reduce ultraviolet penetration; UPF ratings apply to the product, not automatically the whole outfit.

Material transparency: care, safety and environmental impact depend on the specific fabric and manufacture. Broad marketing claims such as “sustainable”, “sculpting” or “supportive” should always be checked against actual product details.

04 · THE PRACTICAL WARDROBE GUIDE

Fit, confidence and care.

Practical advice for choosing, styling and caring for your garments while respecting personal comfort.

Fit the top

Compare underbust and cup measurements. Straps should not do all the work. A top that digs in or rides up needs reconsideration.

Choose coverage freely

Brazilian and thong silhouettes are legitimate fashion choices; select based on comfort and venue requirements, not body 'rules'.

Check fabric wet

Look for suitable lining and opacity when wet, especially with light colours. Try gentle movement before swimming.

Sun safety

A thong or cut-out one-piece leaves more skin exposed. Use broad-spectrum SPF, shade and a cover-up; UPF labels can help with covered areas.

Care after salt and chlorine

Rinse after swimming, avoid twisting delicate pieces and dry in shade according to the care label.

Lingerie hygiene

Use clean personal garments, respect delicate wash instructions and check cotton or comfortable linings and secure closures.

Sensual styling

Layer a lace bodysuit beneath a blazer, combine a satin slip with soft knitwear or coordinate elegant colours without sacrificing comfort.

Buy responsibly

Always review hygiene-related returns and brand-specific fit charts, especially for bikini bottoms and intimate apparel.

LOOKBOOK NOTES

Elegant, expressive, always personal.

A satin robe over lace, a soft shirt over a string bikini, or a pared-back black swimsuit with an oversized linen cover-up: styling is about intention, colour and comfort rather than conforming to a single ideal.

07 · HELPFUL QUESTIONS

Answers without fashion myths.

Common fit, materials and shopping questions answered clearly.

Is a thong bikini suitable for swimming?

It can be swimwear when made from swim-safe, lined material. Test secure fit, adjustability and local beach or pool rules.

What is the difference between Brazilian and thong?

Both describe cuts with less rear coverage than classic briefs. Exact terminology and coverage vary by brand.

Does a tight bikini top give better support?

Not necessarily. Support depends on band, cup fit, construction and activity; a too-tight top can cause discomfort.

Can a lace bodysuit be worn as outerwear?

Yes, for adult fashion styling with appropriate lining, layering and personal preference. Check transparency and fabric durability.

Should I wear lingerie while swimming?

Ordinary lace, silk or cotton lingerie is not designed for salt water or chlorine. Choose purpose-made swimwear.

What is the best swimsuit for my body?

There is no single rule. Fit, comfort, coverage preferences, secure movement and the look you enjoy are more meaningful.

How should I wash delicate lace?

Follow the specific care label. A mesh wash bag and gentle setting may help where machine washing is permitted; avoid high heat.

Does a bikini replace sun protection?

No. Apply sunscreen to exposed skin and use shade, suitable cover-ups and other sun-protection measures.
